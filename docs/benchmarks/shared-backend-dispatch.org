* Reproduced defects and implemented changes

The engine at =5400bb20981f50f4fb3e293fb606bb878fbcc1ce= immediately
submitted every accepted request to the Host blocking pool. A native Turso probe
with two blocking threads, one held validated write and seven queued writes
failed a 500 ms recovery deadline. Accepted read capacity did not prevent the
writer connection's mutex waiters from consuming both threads.

The shared dispatcher now waits asynchronously for separate write/read worker
permits, defaulting to one each. Queue admission still owns its count/byte lease;
caller cancellation cannot abandon an administrative update or owned drain.
This avoids submitting the seven connection waiters to blocking threads.

The initial dispatcher at =c1f40741840cdfa452eb2e7e896d11efb631cc9c=
exposed a second race: recovery concurrency 8 returned =BeforeCommit(Saturated)=
when the predecessor's successful response arrived before its lease was released.
The final dispatcher delivers provider results after releasing accounting and
worker permits. The regular native regression runs 64 commit/recover pairs with
single-slot write/read admission and requires immediate capacity reuse after
success. No admission limit was raised to hide the race.

* Historical recovery while a native writer is stalled

The mixed harness caps its own Host at two asynchronous workers and two blocking
threads. One already-validated native transaction remains held while seven other
writes are accepted. During the entire measured read window, the gate remains
closed, eight writes remain accepted and one write job occupies the dispatcher.
Recovery lanes are 1/4/8, with 32 exact lookups of the committed seed per lane.
There is no read warmup. Three rounds alternate lane-count order.

Rows below are medians of three round-level statistics, with individual-read
nearest-rank p50/p95/p99 in milliseconds. =Drain ms= is released-stall-to-close
wall time, including the eight pending writes. Drain begins while the writer is
still held; canceling that initial 20 ms shutdown waiter must leave drain owned.

All 18 cases passed: 162 fresh commits, 2,496 repeated exact historical reads
while the writer remained stalled, and 162 exact record checks after reopen.
Every accepted write finished during drain. Both close calls converge on Closed,
zero accepted/submitted work, zero retained request bytes and exact completed
counts. The 36 original/reopened Backend instances closed successfully.

These limits govern one Backend's own submissions. Competing Host jobs, other
Backend instances, or a Host with insufficient blocking capacity can still delay
recovery. Neither the dispatcher nor this measurement reserves global OS threads.
